History

1962
Built by Rad Mfg Co · 2240 SE 31st St, Portland
The Viper is fabricated at Rad Mfg Co's shop at 2240 SE 31st Street, Portland, Oregon (phone 235-1669). Constructed to full SCCA Formula Vee specification using VW Beetle running gear on a fully triangulated 1" square 16-gauge mild steel space frame. Body in four-piece fiberglass, impregnated in the standard Viper yellow. The brochure tagline: "The inexpensive way to go racing. Viper — the way to win."
1962–
SCCA Competition — Riviera Team Portland · Race No. 356
The Viper competes in SCCA Formula Vee as #356, entered under the Riviera Team Portland. Period photography shows the car at Oregon International Raceway, in the paddock alongside GT cars of the era. The brochure notes the Viper was proven at OIR: "extremely faster through the corners."

"Formula Vee — the inexpensive way to go racing. Viper — the way to win."

The October 1964 issue of Sports Car Graphic ran a multi-page feature on Formula Vee by Wally Korb that prominently featured the Viper. The article placed FV within American club racing — arguing that the class's reliance on stock VW components made it uniquely accessible, while exploring how competitive the formula could become.

The feature included period photographs of the Viper's bare tubular chassis, its white fiberglass body panels, and the cockpit layout — making it one of the few contemporary publications to document the car's engineering in detail. Photos credited to John Christy, Wally Korb, and Peter Soumac.

That this issue survives with the car is a meaningful piece of provenance: a national press record from the year after the Viper's debut, confirming its visibility in the sports car world during its active racing life. Click any page to enlarge.

Context & Background

About the Car

Formula Vee was established as an SCCA class in 1958, built on the premise that real open-wheel road racing could be made affordable by mandating stock Volkswagen Beetle components — engine, suspension, brakes, and transaxle — in a purpose-built single-seat chassis.

By 1962 the class had taken hold nationally, with constructors appearing not just in Florida and California but across the country. Rad Mfg Co, operating out of 2240 SE 31st Street in Portland, Oregon, was among the Pacific Northwest builders producing FV cars for regional SCCA competition.

The Viper's design was practical and effective: a fully triangulated mild steel space frame, four-piece fiberglass body in factory yellow, and a rear section that could be dropped with the engine and transaxle as a unit for easy paddock servicing. The brochure advertised Koni shocks both front and rear, and springs wound to Rad's own specification — claimed to make the car "extremely faster through the corners" than other FV competitors.

Race #356, entered by the Riviera Team Portland, is one of the documented Vipers to have competed at Oregon International Raceway. The survival of both the car and its original factory brochures makes this a rare and complete piece of Pacific Northwest motorsport history.
